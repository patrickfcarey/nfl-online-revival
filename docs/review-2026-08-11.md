### D1 — the R6 headline metric sums *disjoint* pairings: the baseline 30 is really 15

Severity: **critical**. File: `experiments/double_team.py` — `_role_frames`, `_holds`,
`m_dt_longest_hold`, `m_dt_shortest_hold`. Fixture: `tests/test_madden_lab_runner.py:1367-1378`.

`_role_frames` returns **every** sample index anywhere in the play where that
player's `dt_role` ∈ {0,1,2}. There is no contiguity test and no role-change
boundary. `_holds` then reports `len(frames)` — a whole-play total — as
"(entity, frames held, last frame)", and `m_dt_longest_hold` presents the max as
**"THE HEADLINE (R6): the longest any pairing survives, in frames."**

The project's own fixture makes the error exact, and even documents the split
without drawing the conclusion:

```python
#: ... the tight end's 30 are deliberately split either side of a gap: 2-16
#: as primary and 29-43 as helper, spanning 42 frames.
"player:0:4": {0: list(range(2, 17)), 1: list(range(29, 44))},   # TE, 30
"player:0:8": {0: list(range(27, 36)) + list(range(40, 44))},    # RG, 13
```

and `test_the_measured_holds_are_reproduced` asserts `30`.

* The tight end's celebrated **30-frame hold is 15 frames + 15 frames with a
  12-frame gap of role 5 in between**. The longest uninterrupted pairing on the
  baseline is **15 frames — the headline is 2.0× the truth**.
* The right guard's 13 is 9 + 4 across a 4-frame gap.
* The right tackle's 17 spans a role change 0→1 at frame 10 — two different jobs
  in one number.

**Consequence for work in flight.** R6a's acceptance is "the pairing survives
~60 frames". A patch that produced **four separate 15-frame touches** on one man
would score 60 and pass R6a with nothing changed on screen. This is exactly the
failure mode the module docstring says the file exists to prevent.

Two further problems in the same metric:

* **The population mixes both sides.** `_holds` iterates `_assigned(samples,
  "player:")`, so one pairing yields up to three entries (primary, helper, doubled
  defender). In the fixture the RT's 17 (frames 2-18) and the DE's 17 (frames 2-18)
  are **the same pairing counted from both ends**, as are the RG's 13 and the LB's
  13. So the argument that anchors R6 — *"13, 17 and 30 frames on three blockers…
  a single shared duration constant cannot produce three numbers"* — is really
  *two* pairings (17 and 13) plus one man who paired twice. The spread is entirely
  consistent with one constant.
* **The count is also lossy.** `_holds`' own docstring records that PINE has no
  frame advance and the harness misses frames under load, so even the contiguous
  runs are capture counts, not durations. Capture rates within the single baseline
  run work out to roughly 49% / 71% / 76%.

### D2 — `state-dispatch-table.md` merges two different state machines, and `addresses.yaml` overwrote a correct prior fact to do it

Severity: **high**. Files: `docs/state-dispatch-table.md`, `recon/state_table.py:29`,
`tools/madden_lab/addresses.yaml:79-100`.

`addresses.yaml` used to carry `documented_count: 93` beside `count: 115`. Today's
note resolves the disagreement the wrong way:

> `addresses.yaml:95-100` — "COUNT RESOLVED 2026-08-11 … row 93 is indeed entirely
> stubs … Rows 0..92 is 93 rows, so the documented count is almost certainly
> someone stopping at the first all-stub row. **The table has 115.**"

The binary says 93. Three independent lines, any one sufficient:

1. **The engine's own descriptors.** `0x006012d0` = `{u16 count = 93, u16 = 10,
   ptr = 0x00527238}` and `0x006012e0` = `{u16 count = 22, u16 = 8,
   ptr = 0x00527af0}`. Both tile exactly: `93 × 24 = 0x8B8` and
   `0x00527238 + 0x8B8 = 0x00527af0`; `22 × 24 = 0x210` ends at `0x00527d00`,
   precisely where the function pointers stop. Five sibling tables in the same
   region tile the same way (counts 6/5/6/4/6 × 24 abut the next pointer to the
   byte), so the field really is a row count.
2. **Registration.** `0x001f88a8` installs the 93-row descriptor for machine
   classes **0 and 1** (`0x00166c74` with `a0 = 0`; `0x0016f4ec` with `a0 = 1`);
   `0x001fa578` installs the 22-row one for class **2** (`0x00182dbc`, `a0 = 2`).
   The class-2 record is followed inline by the strings `BALL.C` and `Balls`.
   Class 0's state byte is `player+0xBCC` (`0x00164f8c addiu a1, s0, 3020`), which
   `addresses.yaml` already proves is `ai_state`.
3. **Falsification.** Sweeping every `jal 0x001b02c8` (push_state) in the image and
   recovering the literal id: class 0 pushes 1, 3, 4, 6, 9, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 26,
   27, 30, 31, 33, 40, 41, 47, 52, 61, 73, 87, 90, 91, 92 — **maximum 92, never
   ≥ 93**. Class 2 pushes 3, 9, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17 — all < 22.

Consequences: rows 93..114 are **states 0..21 of another machine**, not states
93..114; "115 of 115 rows hold at least one non-null pointer" is arithmetic about
a boundary that is not there; the 26 shared handlers double-count cross-machine
reuse; and the reasoning that produced the change is itself wrong — the **first**
all-stub row is **48**, not 93.

The generator's guard cannot catch this: it validates two rows *inside* the first
93 and then trusts `ROWS = 115`.

00260700  jal 0x00260688 …             ; preserve bits 18 and 22
00260718  daddu a0, s0, zero

### D5 — the R6 patch direction is derived from a timer that counts *up* in half the cases

Severity: **high**. File: `docs/double-team-requirements.md` ("R6 FINAL TARGET",
"R6 final design").

R6 proposes extending `reselect_timer` (+0x432) — "the base 30 becomes ~90". The
service routine at `0x001f5bd0`-`0x001f5d0c` switches on `engagement` (`+0x3E0`)
and uses the field in **two opposite directions**:

| engagement kind | direction | expiry |
|---|---|---|
| 4, 8 (`in contact`; `second man attached — double team live`) | `addiu v0, v0, -1` — counts **down** | breaks when it passes below 0 (`0x001f5c58`, `0x001f5c90`) |
| 3, 7 (`approaching a ball carrier`; `assigned as second man on a double team`) | `addiu v1, v1, 1` — counts **up** | breaks at **≥ 61** (`0x001f5c20 slti v0, v0, 61`); gates at **≥ 21** (`0x001f5cd0`) |
| default | counts **up**, threshold 11 | — |

Per `addresses.yaml`'s own `engagement_kind` enum, **7 and 8 are the double-team
kinds**. So the helper spends the *approach* on an up-counter with a 61 limit and
the *attachment* on a down-counter. Raising the initialiser from 30 to 90 would
delay the kind-8 expiry as intended **and simultaneously make the kind-7 branch
expire on its first tick** (90 ≥ 61). The stated patch is self-defeating on the
exact transition it targets.

Two errors fall out of the same reading:

* "**Better blockers re-select SOONER** (higher rating subtracts more) … precisely
  backwards from football" is true only on the counting-**down** branches. On the
  counting-up branches a lower initial value means *more* time to reach 61, so the
  better blocker holds **longer**. The claim is stated without the branch.
* `+0x432` has **more than one initialiser**: `0x001efa34 sh s4, 1074(s0)` in
  `0x001ef820` writes it too, and four sites clear it through a `+0x3E0` base. A
  patch to one constant will not cover them.

Independent arithmetic: ratings are the 0..255 effective scale, so `30 − rating/16`
spans **15..30**. It cannot produce a 13 — which is separately explained by D1.

### D7 — `m_carrier_yards` returns a near-constant −1.6 on every iteration

Severity: **high** (wrong on every run today). File: `experiments/pass_protection.py`.

```python
forward = _forward_sign(samples, los)          # +1 here
deepest = max(ys) if forward > 0 else min(ys)
return float((deepest - los) * forward)
```

`max(ys)` is the most-**downfield** point the carrier reached. On a held-ball
dropback the QB never crosses the LOS, so `max(ys)` is pinned at his snap position
(QB_SPOT y = 13.4, LOS = 15.0) and the metric returns **≈ −1.6 regardless of whether
the sack came at 3 yards deep or 12** — while the docstring claims it is "net yards"
that "tells us how long protection actually held", with `higher_is="better"`.
Absolute error ≈ 5-9 yd against real sack depth, and — worse for an A/B harness —
**zero variance**: it can never distinguish two arms. Net yards is `ys[-1] − los`;
sack depth is `min(ys) − los`.

Second, smaller defect in the same metric: `carrier_y` follows
`ball.carrier or ball.last_carrier`, and pre-snap `+0xB4` is null, so early samples
return the **halfback** and later ones the QB. `max` is taken over a series that
changes subject mid-play.

### D8 — `_decay_fraction` computes a range, not a decay

Severity: **medium-high**. File: `experiments/pass_protection.py`.

```python
top = max(values)
return float((top - min(values)) / top)
```

`max` and `min` are order-independent, but the docstring says "how much of its
**starting** value the component **loses**". A rep whose `contest_power` only ever
*rises* — a mid-episode recompute, which `m_recompute_steps` exists to count and
`_steps` proves happens inside episodes — reports a large "decay fraction". A
blocker recomputed 500 → 1050 inside one rep reports `decay_within_rep = 0.52`, a
52% collapse where the value went up, and `higher_is="worse"` then scores that
inverted reading as the worse outcome. The error is one-sided (never negative) so
it never announces itself.

### D10 — `0x001f6b0c` was refuted for the wrong reason, and the right reason matters

Severity: medium-high. File: `docs/double-team-requirements.md:366-412`.

The commit trail is right that patching `0x001f6b0c` changes nothing about duration,

`0x005fef3c` does have exactly one reader (`0x001e8218`, verified with a
gp-aware scan). But the mystery built on it — a state-33 player reading exactly
0.4600, therefore "whatever writes it is shared" — has a mundane answer:

* The float **0.46 appears at 50 distinct addresses** in the image, and **49 of them
  are referenced by code**. `0x005fef3c` is one copy of a very common constant.
* `speed_cmd` (`+0x1E8`) has **311 non-stack store sites**.

The plan's conclusion ("not a safe knob") is right; the argument should be "one of
50 copies of a common constant, into a field with 311 writers", not "sole reader".
As written, `:128` (sole reader) and `:209-211` (whatever writes it is shared)
contradict each other inside one document and neither is withdrawn.

### D12 — DT-3's polarity is documented ambiguously, and the ambiguity inverts the patch

Severity: medium. File: `docs/double-team-plan.md:31-37,129`.

"DT-3 `0x001F4AE8` — helper assignment is **gated on play type 2**" reads as
*requires* type 2. The code is the opposite:

```
001f4adc  jal   0x0015ada0
001f4ae4  addiu v1, zero, 2
001f4ae8  beq   v0, v1, 0x001f4c04   ; 0x001f4c04 is the FUNCTION EPILOGUE
```

The enclosing function starts at `0x001f4790` (`addiu sp, sp, -256`) and
`0x001f4c04`-`0x001f4c38` is its epilogue (`addiu sp, sp, 256`; `jr ra`). Value 2 is
an **exclusion** — it returns immediately. The proposed patch (branch → `nop`)
removes the exclusion, which is the intended effect, so the patch is right and the
sentence is wrong. That is the dangerous combination: anyone who later "corrects"
the patch to match the prose will invert it. (Still open, and the plan's own step 4
flags it: `0x0015ada0`'s return may be the same `block_mode` enum as DT-1, in which
case DT-3 *is* DT-1.)

### D13 — `_assigned` has no domain check on a `doc`-grade byte

Severity: medium. File: `experiments/double_team.py` — `_assigned`.

`dt_role` is `+0x437`, `confidence: doc`. The test is `!= 5`, so **any** other value

### D16 — the frame-index / `frames_since_snap` distinction the specs document does not exist

Severity: medium. Files: `experiments/double_team.py` (`m_dt_first_frame`,
`m_dt_last_hold_frame`), `experiments/pass_protection.py`.

`World` has **no `frame_counter()`** — `runner.py:209` sets `degraded = True` when
it is missing and `__main__.py:208-212` reports its absence as SEAM REQUEST 4. The
clock falls back to `frames_since_snap`, `base_counter` is read after a load confirm
that *requires* the counter to be 0, and `snapshot.index = snapshot.tick -
base_counter`. Therefore **`frame.index == frames_since_snap`, exactly**.

`m_dt_first_frame`'s docstring instructs the reader to add "the pre-snap frames the
iteration spent before the snap at script frame 4" before comparing against DT-2's
60-frame gate. Doing so over-corrects by ~4+ frames and could flip a registration at
frame 58 (inside the gate) to 62 (outside) — reversing the finding. The pre-snap
frames are not in the index at all, because the counter does not move pre-snap. The
test fixture bakes the fiction in (`("game","frames_since_snap"): max(0, i - 4)`).

### D17 — `worst_drop_early` vs `worst_drop_late` compares maxima over unequal windows

Severity: medium. File: `experiments/pass_protection.py`.

The window arithmetic is right (`[0,60)` = before 60; `[150,∞)` = at or after 150).
The **comparison** is not: early is a fixed 60-frame window, late runs from 150 to
wherever the play ends (bounded only by `max_frames=480`) and varies per iteration.
A maximum grows with window length and with the number of steps inside it, so the
headline signature "late ≫ early" is confounded with exposure. Nothing normalises
it — there is no `steps_early` / `steps_late` count. Compounding it, a `None` from
`worst_drop_late` conflates three situations (the play never reached 150; nobody was
engaged after 150; engaged with no downward step) and only the first is
disambiguated.

Related, same file: `_steps` differences **consecutive samples** while the metric is
named "largest **single-step** fall". The harness drops frames under load, so two
decay applications separated by a dropped poll merge into one apparent step —
which, since the test is "does this step exceed the ramp's ceiling", can manufacture
the evidence that refutes the mechanism. No metric reads the `dropped` count. And
the ceiling figures themselves (0.32 early, 0.95 late) assume both
`jal 0x001532d0(ramp*0.95, byte)` and the subsequent random return ≤ 1.0, which the
quoted listing does not establish.

## 5. The generalisation

The archetype the `m_defender_pushback` bug came from is not "whole play vs
episode". It is **"the scope the metric names is not the scope the expression
walks"**, and it recurs here in four disguises:

1. **Aggregating a set of scoped frames as if it were one interval** — D1, D3.
   `_role_frames` returns a *set*; `len()` and `[0]`/`[-1]` treat it as a *run*.
2. **Using an order-independent aggregate where the claim is directional** — D7, D8.
   `max`/`min` cannot express "loses from its starting value" or "net yards".
3. **Setting the episode boundary at a threshold that admits a different behaviour
   than the one being measured** — D9. `>= 2` is not "engaged".
4. **Comparing two maxima taken over windows of unequal length** — D17.

The binary-side equivalent, which cost two of the day's findings, is: **a negative
proved by matching a literal argument or a literal displacement is not proved.**
D4 (an index-passing setter) and the `+0x42C` / `+0x432` scans (a biased base
register) both failed the naive form and passed the wide one.
